Political Thought Guide

A standing work of reference maintained by the Foundation and kept apart from the articles. Six eras, 48 schools, and 123 thinkers, each entered with dates, canonical works, a portrait, and a compact statement of the argument for which the thinker is read.

Consult the guide

Featured Thinkers

  • Aristotle

    384–322 BCE

    Founded the comparative study of constitutions, and held that the city exists for the good life rather than for life alone.

  • John Stuart Mill

    1806–1873

    Set the harm principle against coercion by the state and against the despotism of custom alike.

  • Hannah Arendt

    1906–1975

    Recovered politics as action among plural equals, and traced totalitarianism to loneliness and superfluousness.

  • B. R. Ambedkar

    1891–1956

    Held that caste is graded inequality beyond the reach of reform, and that democracy must deliver one person, one value in society as well as in law.

Submissions
